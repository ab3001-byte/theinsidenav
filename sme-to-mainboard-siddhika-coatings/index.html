Siddhika Coatings: the full kundali

The wall you have walked past

Walk into a new hospital, an IT park or a premium mall and look at the outer wall. It is often not flat paint. It has a grainy, stone-like texture that holds its colour for years in Indian sun and rain.

Someone specified that finish. Someone supplied the material. And someone with a trained crew applied it, coat by coat, on scaffolding forty feet up. On many such projects, that someone is a Delhi company most investors have never heard of.

Siddhika Coatings was set up in Delhi in July 2010. It became a public company in January 2020 and listed on NSE's SME platform in March 2021, raising just ₹4.7 Cr in its IPO. Today it is worth about ₹130 Cr.

The business: a contractor with a Japanese brand

The heart of Siddhika is one relationship. It is the strategic channel partner in India for SK Kaken of Japan, and an authorised importer of its products through SK Kaken's Singapore arm. SK Kaken is one of Japan's largest makers of building coatings, with roughly half of that country's architectural coatings market.

Siddhika brings those coatings to India and applies them on site with its own teams. It describes itself as India's largest single brand paint contract company, and handles over 5 million sq. ft. of contract work a year.

A typical job runs like this:

1

An architect or builder specifies a premium textured finish for a project.

2

Siddhika imports the coatings. In FY24, imports were ₹7.1 Cr, mostly paid in Singapore dollars.

3

Its trained crews apply the finish on site, and the company is paid in stages.

4

Banks issue performance guarantees on its behalf. In FY24 these stood at ₹5.6 Cr, fully backed by fixed deposits.

This is why its margins look unusual for a contractor. Siddhika is not a generic painter competing on price. It sells a branded Japanese system together with the skill to apply it properly. Operating margin has risen from 9% in FY22 to 22% in FY26.

The company has offices in Delhi, Kolkata, Bangalore, Hyderabad and Mumbai, and about 90 permanent employees. It does not name its customers. Its annual report only says repeat clients are its main strength, which is the first thing we would ask management to explain.

Industry and moat: a niche the giants overlook

India's paint industry is in a price war. Birla Opus has entered with about ₹10,000 Cr of investment, and JSW Paints now controls Akzo Nobel India. Competition runs from economy to luxury.

For Siddhika, this matters less than it seems. The large brands fight for the dealer counter and the home painter. Siddhika wins work through architects and builders on projects, and then does the application itself. A war at the dealer counter does not reach it directly.

What protects it
  • The SK Kaken name, which architects specify
  • Application skill: textured systems fail when applied badly
  • A 15 year record and repeat clients
What could weaken it
  • SK Kaken deciding to sell and apply directly in India
  • Large brands moving into project contracts
  • Terms of the SK Kaken agreement (tenure, exclusivity) are not disclosed

The Mizutani development

Siddhika has announced a joint venture with Mizutani Paint, a 100 year old Osaka company that makes roof, wall and floor coatings. That is the same space as SK Kaken.

There are two ways to read this. It may be a sensible step to reduce dependence on one supplier. Or it may strain the SK Kaken relationship. The JV is also expected to cost money with no sales for about 12 months. How SK Kaken views this partnership is the most important question for management today.

Management and governance

Promoters hold 63.09%, unchanged since September 2021, with no pledge reported. They have not sold a share in five years. Managing Director Charitra Maheshwari has more than 28 years in business and has led the group for over a decade.

The numbers: quality is high, growth has paused

Between FY21 and FY26, sales grew about 3.3 times while operating margin went from 7.4% to 21.9%. Profit grew much faster than sales because the business got better, not just bigger.

The latest half year tells a different story. Sales for April to September 2026 were ₹28.2 Cr, up only 3.7% on the year before. Net profit dipped slightly, from ₹4.80 Cr to ₹4.73 Cr. Margins held near 21%, so the business is not weakening. It has simply stopped growing for now.

Over FY22 to FY26, the company converted 77% of its operating profit into operating cash. Free cash flow was positive every year. For a contractor, where customers often pay late, that is a healthy record.

Three things to keep an eye on. Inventory days are high at 96, because imported material is stocked ahead of jobs. Other income of about ₹1.8 Cr comes from the investment pile, about 13% of pre tax profit. And that pile keeps growing: ₹30.75 Cr in September 2026, up from ₹13.6 Cr in March 2024. Management is saving cash rather than reinvesting it. The JV may be where it goes next.

For a debt free business earning over 30% on its capital, under 12 times core profit is not demanding. Listed paint majors trade at far higher multiples, but they are large, liquid brands. Siddhika is small, thinly traded and dependent on one supplier, so a discount is fair.

SME to Mainboard check: not yet

NSE allows SME companies to move to its main board once they meet a set of tests, revised from May 2025. Siddhika passes four of the six.

Paid up capital is an easy gap to close. A 1:1 bonus would take it from ₹6.18 Cr to ₹12.36 Cr, and the company has done a bonus before.

Revenue is the real gap. Sales need to reach ₹100 Cr in a single year.

At 12% a year, Siddhika crosses ₹100 Cr in about five years. At 18%, in about three and a half. The latest half year grew at 4%. Unless growth picks up meaningfully, migration is a 2030s event.

A good SME is not automatically a mainboard candidate. Quality gets you noticed. Scale gets you promoted.Atharva

What could change the picture:

1

Details of the Mizutani JV: capital, structure and first products launched in India.

2

Sales growth back above 15% for two half years in a row.

3

A bonus issue, which is needed for migration.

4

Any clarity on the SK Kaken agreement, its renewal or exclusivity.

And what would break the thesis: a split between Siddhika and SK Kaken, or margins falling back below 15% while sales stay flat. In that case, the company would be valued mainly on its cash and dividend.

Our read

Siddhika is one of the cleaner businesses on India's SME exchanges. It earns high returns, carries no debt, turns profit into cash and pays a dividend. At about 12 times core profit, the market is not asking much for that quality.

What the market is waiting for is growth. The last six months showed almost none, and the Mizutani JV will cost money before it earns any. For our SME to Mainboard tracker, Siddhika is a quality business to follow closely, not a near term migration story. We will revisit it when the JV details and the next half year results are out.
