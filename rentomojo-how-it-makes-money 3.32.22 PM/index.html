How Rentomojo actually makes money

You pay rent for the fridge every month. But Rentomojo paid for it on day one. That small gap is the whole story.

Atharva26 September 2026, 7 min read

Rahul just got his first job in Bengaluru.

He stays in a PG for a month. Then he finds a nice flat. He opens the door and it is completely empty. No bed. No sofa. No washing machine.

He does the maths. Bed, sofa, washing machine, maybe a TV. Close to ₹1 lakh.

Then a thought hits him. What if he switches jobs next year? What if he moves back home? ₹1 lakh on furniture he may sell cheap on OLX in twelve months? No thanks.

So Rahul opens Rentomojo. He picks what he needs. He pays a small amount every month. If he moves, he just stops paying.

Two problems, solved. No big payment at the start. No baggage at the end.

Lakhs of young Indians do exactly this. So far, very simple. The interesting part starts now.

The latecomer who won

Here is the twist. Rentomojo did not start this idea. Furlenco was renting furniture online before Rentomojo even existed. Local shops had done it offline for years.

Yet look where Rentomojo stands today.

50%+of subscribers in organised rental are with Rentomojo
42 to 47%of the market's revenue

So how did the latecomer win? Not with a prettier app. With something you and I never see when we open it.

Every fridge is a bet

When Rahul rents a fridge, Rentomojo has already paid the full price for it. Rahul pays it back slowly. A little every month.

Think about it. Rentomojo is basically giving an EMI. Only difference, the customer never owns the product.

So every fridge is a bet on future rent. It must stay rented. Month after month. Customer after customer. A fridge sitting in a godown earns nothing, and that day's rent never comes back.

The boring machine behind the app

Rentomojo calls it a three layer flywheel. In simple words, it does three jobs really well.

1

Deliver and look after itDelivery, installation, doorstep repair, shifting houses and pickup. All on one logistics network.

2

Keep the customer longerThe average customer stays for about 18 months.

3

Bring it back and rent it againReturned items get fixed, cleaned and sent out to the next customer.

That third job is the secret. In FY26, Rentomojo rented out 8.5 lakh items. In the same year, it refurbished 6 lakh items. So for every 10 items going out, about 7 old items got a second life.

Old stock is not dead stock here. It is stock waiting for its next Rahul.

Being first is nice. Keeping your fridges busy is what pays.Atharva

The 42% that gets everyone excited

In FY26, Rentomojo made ₹387 Cr in revenue. Its EBITDA was ₹163 Cr. That is a margin of about 42%. For a consumer business, that is a very big number.

But wait. EBITDA is profit before interest, tax and depreciation. Depreciation simply means your stuff getting old.

For an app company, depreciation is a few laptops. For Rentomojo, the whole business is stuff. Fridges get old. Sofas get old. In FY26, depreciation was ₹70 Cr. That is about 43% of the EBITDA.

EBITDA is the Instagram photo. Cash flow is the passport photo.Atharva

Now, the passport photo

In FY26, the business brought in ₹173 Cr of cash from its operations. It spent ₹176 Cr buying new fridges, beds and sofas.

Paisa aaya, paisa gaya. Actually, slightly more gaya. About ₹3 Cr more.

Is that bad? Not always. If Rentomojo buys 1 lakh new washing machines because more people want them, that is growth. That is good spending.

But some money only replaces old stuff. That spending does not grow anything. It just keeps the shop running.

In plain wordsGrowth spending is like opening a second shop. Replacement spending is like repainting the first one. Both cost money. Only one makes you bigger. How the ₹176 Cr splits between the two decides whether this is a great business or just a busy one.

The profit jump, with an asterisk

Rentomojo's profit after tax went from ₹43 Cr in FY25 to ₹104 Cr in FY26. That is 2.4 times. Party time?

Not so fast. About ₹37 Cr of that is a deferred tax credit.

Simple meaning. Rentomojo made losses in its early years. Tax rules let it use those old losses to pay less tax later. Accounting lets it count that future benefit today. It is real, but it is not money earned by renting fridges this year.

Remove the ₹37 Cr and the business earned roughly ₹67 Cr. Still up about 56% from last year. A good jump. Just not a 2.4 times jump.

The fridge that became a 5 bagger

This is my favourite part of the whole story.

Rentomojo tracks its assets by the year it bought them. Like a batch of students. Then it checks how each batch did over the years.

Things bought in FY17 have earned 5.1 times their cost in rent by FY26. And 56.1% of them are still out there, earning. The FY18 batch has earned 4.5 times its cost, with 60.9% still working.

In market bhasha, the FY17 batch is a 5 bagger. From a fridge.

One honest noteEarning 5 times its cost in rent is not 5 times profit. Delivery, repairs, staff and interest all cost money. But it does show one thing. The accounts say the fridge is getting old. The data says the old fridge is still working.

So what is Rentomojo, really?

Look at it one way, and it is a subscription business. Loyal customers, monthly bills, a 42% margin. Looks premium.

Look at it another way, and it is an equipment rental company. Depreciation, capex, borrowing costs, product life. Looks ordinary.

The truth sits in between. It is a subscription business that has to keep buying its own shelf.

What I would keep an eye on

1

Busy or idleHow much of the stuff is rented out at any time.

2

How long things lastA sofa lasts years. A washing machine? Appliances are the real test.

3

Cost of refurbishingFixing old stuff must stay much cheaper than buying new.

4

Growth or replacementHow much of the capex adds new customers.

My take

Rentomojo has built a clever machine. It buys once and rents many times. The old batches prove it can work.

But this machine eats cash to grow. If the stuff stays busy and lives long, the maths is lovely. If not, 42% is just a nice filter.

Appliances will decide the ending. Time will tell.
