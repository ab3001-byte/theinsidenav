Dr. Anjali forwarded the deck at 11 pm with one line. "Is this lion real, or just wearing sunglasses?"

She is 49, an eye surgeon in Baner. Over 15 years she has built about ₹2.4 crore in mutual funds, almost all in large cap and flexi cap funds. Her portfolio is solid and a little sleepy. Her CA had suggested she add a mid and small cap slice, and a PMS team had sent her a 48 page presentation for AAA Budding Beasts.

"The numbers look too good," she said on the call next morning. "That's usually when I get suspicious."

Fair instinct. So we did what we do with every product. We read the deck end to end, then went looking for what the deck does not say.

Dr. Anjali is a made up name. The question is real, and I hear it every time a strong performer lands in someone's inbox.

First, what are we looking at?

A PMS is a portfolio a professional manages for you, but the shares sit in your own demat account. Minimum ticket is ₹50 lakh by SEBI rule.

Budding Beasts is the mid and small cap strategy of AlfAccurate Advisors (AAA), a SEBI registered portfolio manager founded 16 years ago. The idea is in the name. Buy companies that are already leaders in their small corner, before they become big.

What we liked, honestly

I'll start with the good, because there is a lot of it.

1. The person who owns the firm runs the money

Rajesh Kothari has 32 years in Indian markets. In the mid 2000s he managed equity funds at DSP Merrill Lynch that were rated among the best in the country. He later worked at a US based India fund. Today he is both the founder and the chief investment officer.

This matters more than people think. When the founder's name is on the door and his own reputation is in the portfolio, decisions tend to be careful. There is also no broking arm or wealth desk pushing products. The firm only manages money.

2. The filter starts with profit, not stories

Out of about 4,800 listed companies, AAA first keeps only those making at least ₹50 crore of yearly profit. That leaves 1,336. Then come checks on cash flow and debt, which leaves 638. The final portfolio is 30 to 50 names.

Today, 95.7% of the portfolio companies make over ₹50 crore profit a year. About 41% have no net debt at all. This is a "small but already profitable" portfolio, not a "small and hopeful" one. That is a real difference from many small cap PMS products.

In plain wordsThey don't buy tiny companies with a dream. They buy mid sized companies that already make solid money and have room to grow. Think of a bearing maker, a diagnostics chain, or a pipe company that leads its niche.

3. The returns are real, and they are after fees

Here is the yearly record, as reported by AAA. Every number is after fees and expenses.

Since launch, that works out to 23.6% a year, against 12.7% for the BSE 500. Five year rolling returns, which smooth out lucky start dates, have a median of 23.2%. PMS Bazaar's independent listing shows similar numbers: 21.1% a year over five years and 20.4% over three years, to September 2026.

This is a 5.8 year record that covers a war, a rate hike cycle, a huge rally and a long correction. That is not a short sample.

4. It fell less when markets fell

This is the slide I respect most, because it compares the PMS with mid and small cap indices, not just the BSE 500.

In both falls, Budding Beasts lost less than the mid and small cap indices. In the long correction from September 2024, it was almost flat while small caps lost nearly 10%. For an investor, a smaller fall is often worth more than a bigger rally, because it is easier to stay invested.

A good PMS is not the one that runs fastest in a rally. It's the one you can still hold when everyone else is selling.Atharva

Now, the fine print

No product is perfect, and the deck is a sales document. Here is what we found when we pushed on it.

1. The benchmark makes the gap look bigger

The portfolio is about 82% mid and small caps. Its official benchmark, the BSE 500, is mostly large caps. So in a small cap rally, it will almost always "beat" the benchmark.

To be fair to AAA, SEBI's rules push PMS managers to use broad benchmarks, so this is not their trick. But look at the rally in the chart above. Against the Midsmallcap 400 index, the lead in that rally was about 4 points, not 19. The real edge of this PMS is in protecting the downside, not in beating small caps on the way up.

2. The trophies belong to the elder brother

The deck opens with "Best 10 Year Performance Award, four times". Read the small text. Those awards went to AAA IOP, the firm's older multi cap strategy. Budding Beasts is under six years old, so it can't win a 10 year award yet.

And IOP's own recent record is weak. As per PMS Bazaar, it returned −0.9% over one year and −3.9% a year over two years, to September 2026. The awards say something good about the firm. They say nothing direct about this strategy.

3. You are paying a premium price for premium companies

Price to earnings48.9xvs 24.3x for BSE 500
Profit growth, FY2625.1%vs 8.0% for BSE 500
Return on equity16.2%vs 13.9% for BSE 500

The portfolio's companies are growing profits three times faster than the market, and they earn better returns. But you pay twice the market's price for them.

That's fine as long as the growth keeps coming. AAA expects about 26% earnings growth for FY27 and FY28. Those are estimates. If growth slows to the market's pace, a 49x price tag can shrink fast, even if the companies are fine.

4. The last two years have been slower

1 year9.3% vs −7.2% for BSE 500
2 years, per year6.5% vs −1.1%
3 years, per year18.6% vs 9.9%
5 years, per year19.4% vs 8.7%

It still beat the market in every period. But 6.5% a year over the last two years is a reminder that this is equity, not a fixed deposit. October 2026 alone was −6% for the PMS, against −0.9% for the BSE 500. Its ups and downs are also slightly bigger than the broad market's (15.6% vs 14.3% volatility).

5. Half the deck is about India, not about this fund

Slides 24 to 44 cover oil, GDP, inflation, war history and FII flows. Good charts. But they apply to every fund in India. When a deck spends more pages on the economy than on its own portfolio, I read it as "please feel optimistic". Useful context, not a reason to invest.

For comparison, a regular plan mid cap mutual fund costs about 1.7% a year, or ₹85,000 on ₹50 lakh.

So yes, it costs more. The good news is that every return number above is already after fees. The 23.6% is what clients actually earned, not a gross number with fees hiding in the footnote. Also ask about portfolio turnover. In a PMS, every profitable sale is taxed in your name that year.

Our verdict, on the Savitr framework

Every product we recommend goes through the same six checks: People, Parent, Process, Portfolio, Performance and Price. Each gets a score out of 5. The weighted total decides the tier.

The weighted score comes to 4.0 out of 5. No red flags. That puts it in our Silver tier, which means we recommend it when it suits the investor's size, horizon and existing portfolio.

Right now, Budding Beasts is the highest scoring PMS on our list. What keeps it from Gold is mostly price: the fees, and the 49x valuation of what it owns. If it holds its downside protection through the current choppy phase and valuations cool, it can move up.

Who this can suit

It can fit if you
  • Have ₹2.5 crore or more invested, so ₹50 lakh is about 20% or less
  • Already have a large cap and flexi cap core
  • Want mid and small cap exposure with a quality filter
  • Can stay invested for 5 years or more
Skip it if you
  • Would be putting most of your money in
  • Need this money in the next 3 to 4 years
  • Can't watch a 20% fall without selling
  • Want the lowest cost. A mid cap fund is cheaper.

What Dr. Anjali did

She has ₹2.4 crore invested and no mid or small cap exposure. A ₹50 lakh slice is about one fifth of her money. Her horizon is 10 years. On paper, she is exactly the investor this product is built for.

She went ahead with ₹50 lakh, moved from her overlapping large cap funds. We picked the fee option after looking at both side by side. And we wrote down two rules before she signed.

1

We judge it against the Midsmallcap 400 index, not the BSE 500. That is the honest yardstick for what she owns.

2

We review it every year, and seriously at year three. If it lags the mid and small cap index after fees for three years running, we revisit.

"So the lion is real," she said.

"The lion is real," I said. "Just make sure you're paying for the lion, not for the sunglasses."

My take

AAA Budding Beasts is one of the more convincing mid and small cap PMS strategies I have studied. An experienced founder runs the money, the filters are sensible, and the record is long enough to mean something. It has also held up better than the indices in bad markets.

Its weak spots are clear too. It looks better against a large cap benchmark than against its real peers. The awards are borrowed from a sibling. And today you are paying a high price for its companies.

None of these are deal breakers. They are things to know before you sign, not after.
