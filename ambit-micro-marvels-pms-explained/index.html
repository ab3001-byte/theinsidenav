A micro cap PMS at a market low. Opportunity or trap?

Microcaps are down. A slick deck from Ambit says this is the time. We opened the deck, checked the real track record, and did the fee maths. Here is everything, in plain words.

The 60 second version
  1. Ambit Micro Marvels is a PMS that buys 18 to 20 very small listed companies, worth ₹500 to ₹3,500 crore each.
  2. The idea is strong. Over 19 years, microcaps beat the Nifty. But about 3 in 4 microcaps go nowhere. Picking is everything.
  3. The fund manager has a good stock picking record. The PMS itself is only 2 years old, and it lost about 18% in 2025 while the market went up.
  4. Fees are high. In a normal year, a regular small cap mutual fund can leave you with similar money, with no ₹50 lakh minimum.
  5. It can make sense for a small slice of a large portfolio, for 7+ years, if you can sit through a 30% fall. It is not for most people.

Sameer called me on Tuesday with a PDF.

He is 47 and runs an auto parts unit in Bhosari. Business is good. He has about ₹60 lakh sitting in FDs and liquid funds.

"Atharva, a PMS guy sent me this. Ambit Micro Marvels. He says microcaps have fallen, this is the bottom, and small companies will become big companies. Minimum is ₹50 lakh. Should I put it all?"

I said what I always say. "Let's read it first. Then let's check what the deck doesn't show."

Sameer is a made up name. The question is real, and I get it often when markets fall.

First, what is a PMS?

PMS means Portfolio Management Service. A professional manages your money in stocks, like a mutual fund. But there are big differences.

Who owns the sharesYou do, in your own demat account
Minimum investment₹50 lakh, by SEBI rule
Number of stocksUsually 15 to 30, very focused
FeesHigher than mutual funds, often with a profit share
TaxEvery sale by the manager is taxed in your name, that year
Exiting earlyExit charges, often 1 to 3% in the first years
In plain wordsA PMS gives you a more focused portfolio, held in your own name. In return, you pay higher fees, more tax along the way, and you need at least ₹50 lakh to start.

And what is a microcap?

Line up every listed company in India by size. The top 100 are large caps. 101 to 250 are mid caps. 251 to 500 are small caps.

Everything after 500 is a microcap. Ambit's universe is companies ranked 501 to 1,500. Most are names you have never heard of. A bearing maker in Gujarat. A conveyor parts company in Chennai. A chemical unit in Maharashtra.

Ambit's pitch, explained simply

The deck makes four arguments. Let me give each one fairly.

1. Microcaps made the most money over 19 years

True. But notice the period ends in FY24, near the top of a huge small cap rally. Also, index returns are not what most investors actually got. They had to sit through falls of 40 to 60% on the way.

2. Some small companies become big companies

This is the most honest slide in the deck, and I respect Ambit for showing it.

If you catch one of the 26 that grow up, you make 25 to 44% a year. If you catch one of the 74 that don't, you make about 1% a year for ten years.

In microcaps, the opportunity and the risk are the same slide.Atharva

3. Buy niche leaders with no debt

Ambit looks for small companies that dominate tiny markets, where big players don't bother to compete. Think luggage, opalware, glass lined reactors, bearings, amines. In many of these, the top three players own over 70% of the market.

They also avoid debt. Their research shows microcaps with high debt returned much less than those with low debt over FY14 to FY23.

4. Check the company from outside, not just the CEO

Ambit calls this the "scuttlebutt" approach. Simply put, they don't just listen to management. They talk to ex employees, dealers, suppliers, competitors and customers to find out what is really going on.

I like this process. It is the right way to buy small companies, where annual reports can hide a lot.

The fund manager's record

The PMS is run by Bhargav Buddhadev, a chartered accountant with over 20 years in stock research. Before this, he ran a small cap research series at Ambit's broking arm called "Beachcombing".

The deck lists eight of his picks from 2017 and 2018. Over the next 5 years, they returned about 38% a year on average. Stars include Alkyl Amines (up about 15 times) and GMM Pfaudler (up about 8 times). One miss, Tasty Bite, went nowhere.

One honest noteThese were research recommendations, picked and shown by the company itself. Eight picks, no fees, no taxes, and a strong market. They show skill. They do not show what a ₹50 lakh PMS account would have earned.

The part the deck does not show: actual returns

The deck has no performance page for this PMS. So we looked it up. The strategy started on 29 July 2024. Its benchmark is the BSE 500 TRI.

As of 30 September 2026, the last one year return was about +8%. But over two years, it was about −3.7% a year.

In rupees: if Sameer had put in ₹50 lakh two years ago, he would have roughly ₹46 lakh today.

To be fair, two years is too short to judge a microcap strategy. It launched near a market top, and 2025 was brutal for small companies. But it is also too short to trust the story blindly.

Fees: the silent partner

PMS fees come in options. Common ones for this strategy, as listed on tracking sites:

A

Fixed feeAbout 2.5% of your money every year, whether it makes money or not.

B

Low fixed plus profit shareAbout 1% a year, plus 20% of any profit above a 10% yearly return.

!

Exit chargesAbout 3% if you leave in year 1, 2% in year 2, 1% in year 3.

Now the maths. Here is ₹50 lakh after one year, in three kinds of years.

The fee gap in one year looks small. But it repeats every year, and it compounds. So the PMS must pick clearly better stocks than a similar mutual fund, year after year, to be worth it. In a flat year, the fixed fee eats your money. In a great year, the profit share takes a big bite.

And remember tax. The portfolio changed about 71% of its holdings in the last year. Every profitable sale gets taxed in your name that year. In a mutual fund, you pay tax only when you sell.

Is this really the market low?

The Nifty Microcap 250 index fell about 11% from its September 2026 high by 23 October. Crude oil was near $114. Fear is real.

Lower prices do give better odds than buying at a peak. But a market that is lower is not the same as a market at its lowest. Nobody, including the PMS salesperson and me, knows where the bottom is.

That is why I would never put ₹50 lakh in on one day in microcaps. If you go ahead, ask if you can start with the minimum and add in parts over 6 to 12 months.

Who this can suit

It can fit if you
  • Have ₹3 crore or more invested, so ₹50 lakh is under 20%
  • Already have a solid core in large cap and flexi cap funds
  • Won't need this money for 7+ years
  • Can watch it fall 30 to 40% without selling
Skip it if you
  • Would be putting most of your savings in
  • Need the money for a goal in the next 5 years
  • Checked the value every week during the 2025 fall
  • Just want small cap exposure. A mutual fund does that cheaper.

What Sameer did

Sameer has ₹60 lakh in total. Putting ₹50 lakh of it into 20 tiny companies would mean betting almost everything on one manager and one corner of the market.

So he did not sign. He moved part of his FD money into a simple, diversified plan first. We agreed to look at a microcap PMS again when his total investments are much larger, and when this strategy has a longer record.

That is not a "no" to microcaps. It is a "not like this, not yet."

My take

Ambit Micro Marvels has a thoughtful process and an experienced manager. The idea of buying niche, debt free, well run small companies early is sound.

But it is a young strategy with a weak first two years, high fees, and a ₹50 lakh entry ticket into the riskiest part of the market.

A falling market is a good time to look at microcaps. It is a bad time to stop asking questions.
