Meet Rohan.

Rohan is 42. He lives in Pune, works too hard, and this year his company finally noticed. Bonus: ₹50 lakh.

His daughter Anaya is 11. On Monday she wants to be a doctor. By Wednesday, an astronaut. By Sunday, a YouTuber. Whatever she picks, college is 7 years away.

So Rohan did what most parents do. He walked into his bank.

His relationship manager gave him a very wide smile. "Sir, perfect timing. We have a child plan. Insurance plus investment. Your daughter's future, fully secured." There was a glossy brochure. A girl in a graduation cap smiled from the cover.

Rohan almost signed. Then he called us.

Rohan and Anaya are made up names. The question is very real. We get it almost every week.

The whole plan in one picture

Before the details, here is the shape of it. Three phases. Each one has one job.

Rule 1: Keep "child" products away from your child

When a product has the word child in its name, parents stop asking questions. Banks know this very well.

Most child plans mix insurance with investment. You end up with too little of both.

1

Returns are lowTraditional plans usually give about 4 to 6% a year. Check the IRR in your own benefit illustration. It is printed there.

2

Money gets lockedExit early and a big chunk stays with the insurer.

3

Costs stay hiddenCommissions and charges sit inside the plan. You never see them on a statement.

The one useful feature is the premium waiver. If the parent dies, the plan continues. Nice. But you can buy the same protection for a fraction of the price. Take a pure term plan on your own life. Keep insurance and investment in separate boxes.

In plain wordsSame money. Same 7 years. ChildMax ends about ₹31 lakh ahead of the child plan. That is not a rounding error. That is a year of college.

Rule 2: Invest in her name, not yours

At Savitr we call this plan ChildMax. The money goes into mutual funds in Anaya's own name. Rohan runs it as her guardian until she turns 18.

Why bother? Two reasons. First, the money is ring fenced. Dad cannot quietly use it for a new car. Second, on her 18th birthday the money becomes hers, and an 18 year old with no salary pays very little tax. That part comes later. It is the fun part.

1

Open a bank account in her name. A minor account, with you as guardian. Her birth certificate is the key document.

2

Get her PAN card made. It is easy, and she will need it anyway.

3

Gift the ₹50 L to her account. There is no gift tax between a parent and a child. Keep a simple gift letter on file.

4

Open mutual fund folios in her name. Your KYC as guardian, plus her birth certificate. The money must go from her bank account.

One honest catchUntil she turns 18, any gain from this money is added to your income and taxed in your hands. This is called clubbing. So the rule is simple. We do not sell anything before 18 unless we really have to.

Rule 3: Don't put ₹50 lakh in on one day

Putting the whole bonus into equity on one day is a bet on that one day. If the market falls 15% next month, Rohan loses ₹7.5 lakh on paper and his sleep for real.

So we use an STP, a Systematic Transfer Plan. The ₹50 L first sits in a liquid fund, earning about 6 to 7% a year. Every week, a fixed amount moves into the equity funds. In 26 weeks, all of it is invested.

A small detail that matters. An STP only works inside the same fund house. So each fund's share is parked in that fund house's own liquid fund.

On top of this, Rohan starts a ₹50,000 monthly SIP from Anaya's account. He tops up her account every month. Same four funds, same split.

Put together, about 94% sits in shares and 6% in bonds on day one. Aggressive? Yes. For 7 years of growth, it should be. We make it safer as college comes closer.

One more rule. The four funds come from four different fund houses. Otherwise you end up buying the same stocks four times and calling it diversification.

Age 16 and 17: protect mode

The biggest risk is not a bad year. It is a bad year right before the first fee. Imagine the market falls 30% the month college asks for money. You are forced to sell at the bottom.

So the plan changes gear.

16

The SIP goes safe. The same ₹50,000 now goes into an arbitrage fund. Low risk, and taxed like equity. Nothing is sold, so there is no tax to pay.

17

One year of fees goes safe. We move enough from equity to make the safe bucket ₹35 lakh, one full year of fees. This sale is taxed in Rohan's hands because of clubbing. About ₹1.25 lakh. A small price to sleep well.

Age 18: the birthday that saves tax

On Anaya's 18th birthday, three things happen.

1

Minor becomes major. Her folios change status. She does her own KYC with her PAN and her own bank account. Note: SIPs and STPs in a minor folio stop at 18 until this is done. Do it in the birthday month itself.

2

She files her own tax return. Every year, from now on.

3

The money is legally hers. Not Dad's anymore. So please raise a sensible 18 year old. That part of the plan is on you, Rohan.

Now the tax part. Anaya has no salary. So every year she gets two cushions.

₹1.25 Lof equity gains tax free every year, for everyone
₹4 Lbasic exemption, which she can use against those gains when she has no other income

So about ₹5.25 lakh of gains every year, without any tax. Anything above that is taxed at a flat 12.5% plus cess. That is based on today's rules under the new tax regime.

Age 18 to 21: the spend phase

Now the fees start. The plan pays ₹35 lakh a year for 4 years. That is ₹1.4 crore in total.

1

The safe bucket always holds the next 12 months of feesSo a market crash never touches this year's fee.

2

Fees go out through an SWPA Systematic Withdrawal Plan. A fixed amount lands in her bank account every month.

3

Each year, we refill the bucket from equityIn her name, spread through the financial year, so her tax free limit gets used every single year.

At 22, Anaya graduates with about ₹41 lakh still in her name. A head start for her master's, or her first home. Her call. She is an adult now.

The tax truth, honestly

You may have heard that this trick makes college "tax free". Not quite. We would rather show you the real maths.

₹5.8 Ltotal tax from age 17 to 21, on ₹1.4 Cr of fees. About 4%.
₹7.4 Lwhat the same withdrawals would cost if everything sat in Dad's name
In plain wordsThe tax saving is nice, about ₹1.5 lakh. But it is not the hero. The hero is ₹31 lakh more than the child plan, and a safe bucket before every fee. Tax is the cherry on top.

My take

Rohan did not sign the child plan. He is a little less popular at his bank now.

Anaya knows none of this. She is busy choosing between doctor and YouTuber. Good. That is exactly how it should be.

If you have a lumpsum and a child, this is the plan I would use. Grow it in her name. Walk the money in slowly. Make it safe before college. Let the 18th birthday do the tax work.
