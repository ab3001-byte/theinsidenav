I am not saying we are the star

Let me be very clear. I am not writing this to say "look how great our portfolio is". Two good years do not make anyone a genius. Markets humble everyone, us included.

What I am saying is simpler.

1

If your portfolio fell like the NiftyBe sad. Then ask why you are paying for active funds that move exactly like the index.

2

If your portfolio fell more than the NiftyBe properly sad. Something in the mix needs fixing.

3

If your portfolio beat the NiftyYou can be annoyed at the market. But you cannot be at the peak of sadness. The maths does not allow it.

Do this check yourself, in 10 minutes

1

Pick the right benchmark. A large cap fund is compared with the Nifty 50. A mid cap fund with a mid cap index. A balanced portfolio with a balanced benchmark. Wrong benchmark, wrong conclusion.

2

Pick the same dates. Your portfolio return and the benchmark return must cover the exact same period. Your CAS statement or app usually shows this.

3

Subtract. Your return minus the benchmark return. Positive means your portfolio did better than the market. Negative means it did worse.

4

Check the risk too. Did your portfolio fall more or less than the market in bad months? Beating the market by taking double the risk is not a win.

A little bit about Jensen's alpha

Step 3 above is the quick version. Professionals use a sharper tool called Jensen's alpha.

The idea is simple. If your portfolio is riskier than the market, it should give more than the market anyway. Jensen's alpha checks the return you got above what your risk level should have given you.

In plain wordsPositive alpha means the fund manager, or your adviser, actually added value. Zero alpha means you could have got the same thing from an index fund. Negative alpha means you paid extra to get less.

Most fund factsheets and research sites show alpha for each fund. Look it up for your funds. You may be surprised.
