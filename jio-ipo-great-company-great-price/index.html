Jio is a great company. Is ₹10.3 lakh crore a great price?

India's biggest IPO closes on Friday, 23 October. Everyone in your WhatsApp group is applying. Before you do, let's do the maths together.

The 60 second version
  1. Jio is a brilliant business. 53 crore users, profit up 15% a year, debt almost gone after this IPO.
  2. But the price is full. You pay about 34 times its yearly profit. Airtel, which earns more per user, trades at a similar level.

Rohit called me on Tuesday night.

He is 31, works in an IT company in Hinjewadi, and his office WhatsApp group had gone mad. "Bro, Jio IPO. Everyone is putting ₹2 lakh. Grey market is ₹265 premium. Free money. Should I?"

I asked him one question. "Rohit, if your friend sold you his flat, would you check the price, or just check how famous the building is?"

He laughed. Then he said, "Okay. Let's check the price."

So we did. Here is everything we went through, in the same order.

Rohit is a made up name. The question is very real. I have answered it about forty times this week.

Part 1: Remember the scratch card?

Twelve years ago, you bought data like it was gold. A ₹29 scratch card gave you a few hundred MB for four days. You switched off mobile data to save it.

Then, on 5 September 2016, Jio arrived. Free calls. Data so cheap it felt like a mistake. It reached 10 crore users in about 170 days.

Today Jio has 53.3 crore users. That is more people than live in the whole of America.

In plain wordsNobody doubts the story. Jio changed how India uses the internet. The only question in this post is price.

Part 2: What does Jio earn from you?

Telecom companies track one number above all: ARPU. Average Revenue Per User. Simply, how much one customer pays in a month.

Jio earns about ₹215 a month from each user. Airtel earns about ₹264. That ₹49 gap is the bull case.

Here is the simple maths. Every time Jio raises ARPU by ₹10, it earns about ₹6,400 crore more in a year. Same towers, same network. A big part of that can turn into profit.

And there is room. Indian data is among the cheapest in the world. So the market believes tariffs will keep going up for years.

You are not buying today's Jio. You are buying every future tariff hike, in advance.Atharva

Part 3: Is the business actually making money?

Yes. And it is growing steadily.

51.9%EBITDA margin in FY26. Half of every rupee earned stays before interest and depreciation.
0.36xnet debt to EBITDA in FY26. Very low for a telecom company.

But two numbers made me pause.

1

Return on capital is falling12.5% in FY25. 10.76% in FY26. Jio has spent huge money on 5G, and it is not yet earning much on it.

2

Return on net worth is about 9.4%For every ₹100 of shareholders' money in the business, Jio earned about ₹9.40 last year. Decent. Not dazzling.

Part 4: Where does your money go?

This is the part most people skip. When you apply, your money goes to the company. But for what?

Repaying debt is not bad. It makes the company safer, and it saves interest. After this IPO, Jio's net debt will be close to zero.

But be clear in your head. You are not funding new 5G towers or satellites with this money. You are helping clean up the balance sheet before listing.

Good to knowJio is issuing 27 crore new shares, about 3% of the company. Reliance's stake falls from 66.43% to 64.48%. Meta, Google and the other big investors are not selling a single share.

Part 5: So how much are you paying?

At ₹1,119 a share, the whole of Jio is valued at about ₹10.3 lakh crore. It would rank among India's most valuable listed companies on day one.

Last year it made ₹30,049 crore of profit. So you are paying about 34 times one year's profit. That is the P/E.

Jio, at IPO price~34xP/E on FY26 profit
Bharti Airtel~35xP/E as quoted mid October
A Nifty 50 company~21xrough long term average

So Jio is priced like Airtel. But Airtel earns ₹49 more per user and grew profit much faster in the last three years. One brokerage measure, the PEG ratio, puts Jio at about 2.2 and Airtel at about 0.8. Lower means you pay less for each unit of growth.

And one more number. In FY26, Jio's free cash flow, the money left after all spending on the network, was about ₹14,157 crore. Against a price of ₹10.3 lakh crore, that is about ₹1.40 of free cash for every ₹100 you pay.

In plain wordsJio is not overpriced like a hyped startup. It is fully priced like a great company. The good news is already in the price.

Part 6: What did the early investors make?

You will read online that Meta, Google and the 2020 investors made poor returns. That is not quite right.

In 2020, thirteen big investors put about ₹1.52 lakh crore into Jio. At the IPO price, that stake is worth about ₹3.29 lakh crore.

2.3xMeta and Google, who bought at ₹488 a share. About 14 to 15% a year in rupees.
2.0xKKR, Silver Lake, PIF and others, who bought at ₹549. About 13% a year in rupees.

In dollars, their return is lower, because the rupee has weakened since 2020. Still, these investors entered at less than half today's price. You are entering after that doubling.

Part 7: The 4x question

Rohit's real question was simple. "Can this make my money 4 times?"

For your money to grow 4 times, Jio must be worth about ₹41 lakh crore. My rough estimate puts all of Reliance Industries, Jio's parent, at about ₹16 lakh crore today. So Jio would need to be worth about two and a half Reliances.

So 4x in 10 years needs profit to grow about 15% a year for a full decade, and the market to keep paying 34 times profit at the end. Both together.

If either slips, you are looking at 8 to 11% a year. That is roughly what a fixed deposit and a decent index fund give, with far more ups and downs on the way.

Part 8: What can go right, and what can go wrong

Can go right
  • Tariff hikes push ARPU towards Airtel's level
  • 5G and AirFiber home broadband keep growing fast
  • Satellite internet, AI and enterprise services add new income
  • Near zero debt means more profit reaches shareholders
Can go wrong
  • Tariff hikes get delayed by competition or politics
  • Satellite plans still wait for government approval, and Starlink is a loud rival
  • Heavy reliance on Reliance group companies for distribution
  • Return on capital keeps falling as 5G spending continues

So, should you apply?

I won't give you a buy or sell call here. That decision depends on your money, your goals and your risk. But here is how I helped Rohit think about it. There are only three kinds of applicants.

1

The listing day playerApplies for a quick pop on listing. Fine, if you accept it can also list flat or lower. Treat it as a small bet, not a plan.

2

The 10 year ownerBelieves in the ARPU story and will hold through bad years. Expect returns close to Jio's profit growth, not miracles.

3

The patient watcherSkips the IPO and waits to see how the market values Jio after listing. Big companies often give a second chance.

Rohit picked his lane. I won't tell you which one. But he did not put in ₹2 lakh just because the group chat did.

My take

Jio is one of the best businesses India has built. That part is not up for debate.

But a great company and a great price are two different things. At ₹10.3 lakh crore, you are paying for a lot of future success upfront.

Do the maths. Know which of the three applicants you are. Then decide.
