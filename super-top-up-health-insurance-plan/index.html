Joshi kaka is 82.

He lives two floors below Vinayak in a society in Kothrud. Retired bank officer. Morning walks, evening news, strong opinions about both.

Last month, chest pain at 4 am. Ambulance. ICU. Heart surgery. Twelve days in hospital.

The final bill: ₹40 lakh.

Joshi kaka had a retirement corpus of about ₹1.2 crore. His son broke FDs and sold mutual funds to pay the hospital. A third of a lifetime of savings, gone in two weeks.

At the society meeting, everyone said the same thing. "Arre, what can you do? Nobody can save for something like this."

Vinayak went home and could not sleep. He is 38. His wife Pooja is 36. Their son Arjun is 8. And his parents, Baba (64) and Aai (60), live with them. Baba has had high BP for six years.

Vinayak's only cover is ₹5 lakh from his company. His parents have nothing.

Next morning, he called us.

Vinayak, his family and Joshi kaka are made up names. The policy numbers below are real, taken straight from the brochure and policy wording.

First, fix the thinking

The society was right about one thing. You cannot save for a ₹40 lakh surgery. But they drew the wrong conclusion.

A ₹40 lakh bill is not a savings problem. It is an insurance problem.

Insurance is built for exactly this kind of risk. The chance is low. The damage is huge. Thousands of families each pay a small amount, and the few who get hit are paid in full. That is the whole idea.

In plain wordsYour retirement corpus is for living. Groceries, travel, grandchildren's birthdays. It is not meant to pay for an ICU. That job belongs to a health policy.

Three kinds of cover

Most people know only the first one. The third one is the secret.

1

Base health policyPays from the first rupee. Usually ₹5 to 25 lakh. The more cover you want, the faster the premium climbs.

2

Plain top upPays only when one single bill crosses a limit, called the deductible. Two smaller bills in a year? It may pay nothing.

3

Super top upAdds up all your bills in the year. Once the total crosses the deductible, it starts paying. This is the one you want.

The deductible is just the amount you, or your base policy, must cover first in a year. After that, the super top up takes over.

The difference, in one real year

Say Vinayak's family has a ₹5 lakh base cover and a top up with a ₹5 lakh deductible. In one year:

Mar

Arjun gets dengue. Hospital bill: ₹1.2 lakh. The base policy pays it.

Oct

Pooja needs surgery. Bill: ₹6 lakh. The base policy has only ₹3.8 lakh left.

The word that makes the difference sits in the policy wording itself. HDFC ERGO calls it an aggregate deductible. Aggregate means all the bills in the policy year count together. That one word saved Vinayak ₹1.2 lakh in this example.

What it costs, from the actual premium chart

Here is the part that surprises everyone. For Vinayak, Pooja and Arjun (two adults and one child, age band 36 to 45), a ₹5 lakh deductible and ₹20 lakh cover costs ₹4,400 a year. That is about ₹367 a month. Less than one family dinner out.

For Baba and Aai together (two adults, above 60), the same cover costs ₹12,100 a year. Because Baba has BP, the brochure says a loading of 10 to 30% on the premium is likely. So plan for up to about ₹15,700.

The deductible trick nobody talks about

A bigger deductible makes each lakh of cover much cheaper. Look at one adult aged 36 to 45, straight from the chart.

The catch? Someone has to pay that ₹5 lakh deductible. That is where the base policy, or a small fund, comes in. Which brings us to the rule that matters most.

The golden ruleSet your super top up deductible equal to your base cover. Base of ₹5 L, deductible of ₹5 L. No gap in between, nothing paid from your pocket.

The fine print that bites

Every policy has a "what we don't pay" list. This one is long. Here are the parts that matter most for Vinayak's family.

BP and sugar linked problems

If BP or diabetes already exists, its complications wait 36 months. The wording lists coronary artery disease under BP. That means Baba's heart.

Ambulance

Not covered. Keep a few thousand aside.

Consumables

Gloves, masks, diapers, attendant charges and about 200 such items are on a "not payable" list. Keep a small buffer.

Maternity

Pregnancy and childbirth are not covered here.

Treatment abroad

India only. Claims are paid in rupees.

Hiding an illness

The wording says the policy becomes void and premiums are forfeited. Declare everything. Always.

One honest noteThis brochure is an older version. Premiums today are likely higher, and some rules have changed since. For example, current IRDAI rules require insurers to cover mental illness, which this old wording excludes. Always ask for the latest brochure and wording before you buy.

Vinayak's plan

Here is what we built. Simple, and it fits in a WhatsApp message.

1

A personal base policy of ₹5 lakh for the family. His company cover stays, but it vanishes the day he switches jobs. A personal base covers the deductible no matter what.

2

Family super top up: ₹5 L deductible, ₹20 L cover. ₹4,400 a year from the brochure chart. Total family cover: ₹25 lakh.

3

Parents' super top up: ₹5 L deductible, ₹20 L cover. ₹12,100 a year, up to about ₹15,700 with Baba's BP loading. Medical tests first, since both are above 55.

4

A ₹5 lakh "deductible fund" for the parents. Parked in a liquid mutual fund, in a separate folio, never touched for anything else. It pays the deductible if Baba or Aai is hospitalised.

5

Buy this month. Baba's BP clock is 36 months. Every month of delay pushes his heart cover further away.

The super top ups together cost about ₹20,000 a year. Roughly ₹1,700 a month to protect five people and one retirement corpus.

Now, the ₹40 lakh test

Fast forward. Baba is 68, past all waiting periods, and needs the same surgery Joshi kaka had. ₹40 lakh. What happens?

This is the honest part. Vinayak's plan turns a ₹40 lakh disaster into a ₹15 lakh problem. Much better than Joshi kaka. But not good enough.

The fix is simple. Newer super top up plans in the market offer much bigger covers, up to ₹1 crore and more, over the same ₹5 lakh deductible. So before buying, Vinayak will compare the latest plans and aim for ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore of total cover. Same idea. Bigger shield.

In plain wordsThe plan is right. The size needs to match today's hospital bills. Check the biggest cover a policy offers before you fall for a cheap premium.

When a claim happens, do this

1

Tell the insurer on time72 hours before a planned admission. Within 24 hours of an emergency one.

2

Tell the super top up insurer tooThe moment a bill might cross the deductible, inform them, with a copy of what you told the base insurer. People miss this and claims get stuck.

3

Keep every paperBills, reports, discharge summary. For reimbursement, submit within 30 days of discharge.

Your 15 minute checklist

1

Add up your cover todayCompany plus personal. If it is ₹5 to 10 lakh, a normal stay is fine, a serious one is not.

2

Do not depend only on company coverIt leaves with the job. Usually when you need it most.

3

Pick a super top up, not a plain top upLook for the words "aggregate deductible".

4

Match the deductible to your baseNo gap between the two policies.

5

Cover your parents now, at whatever ageEvery year you wait, more of their history becomes "pre existing" and starts a fresh waiting clock.

6

Read five things in the wordingBiggest cover, room rent limit, waiting periods, co pay, and the not payable list.

My take

Joshi kaka did nothing wrong. He saved well, for decades. He just asked his savings to do a job they were never built for.

Vinayak spent one Sunday morning on this. He now pays about ₹1,700 a month, and Baba's surgery, if it ever comes, will not decide Aai's retirement.

That is the cheapest peace of mind an Indian family can buy.
