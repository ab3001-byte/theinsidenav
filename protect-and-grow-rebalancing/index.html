Two retirements, one difference

Mr. Kulkarni and Mr. Patwardhan worked at the same engineering company in Pune, a few years apart in age. Each retired with ₹5 crore. Each chose the same funds. Each decided to take out ₹30 lakh at the start of every year to live on.

Over their first ten years of retirement, both portfolios earned exactly the same set of returns: nine good years of 12%, and one bad year of minus 20%. Same average. Same funds. Same withdrawals.

The only difference was which year of their journey the bad year fell in. Mr. Kulkarni retired after a good run, and his bad year came in year ten. Mr. Patwardhan retired just before a crash, like the one in 2008, and his bad year came in year one.

Neither picked bad funds. Only the timing was different.

Mr. Patwardhan ended ₹1.42 crore poorer.

Here is why. After the crash in year one, he kept taking ₹30 lakh out of a smaller pot. So his nine good years had less money to grow. Mr. Kulkarni's crash came after the growth was already built.

Now here is the part most people miss. If neither of them had withdrawn anything, both would have ended at exactly ₹11.09 crore. So the risk is not the crash itself. The risk is being forced to sell during the crash. Protect & Grow is built to remove that forced sale.

Every family that lives off its portfolio faces this risk. The question is how to remove that forced sale without giving up growth.

What about keeping 100% in equity? In a normal crash it ends higher, at ₹6.65 crore with withdrawals. But it falls to ₹3.53 crore in year one, which is hard to sit through. Protect & Grow, in simple words

Protect & Grow is the rule based way we hold portfolios at Savitr. It does two jobs. It keeps the money you need for the next few years safe, so you never sell equity in a crash. And it uses fixed rules to buy equity when it is cheap and trim it when it is expensive.

The portfolio is split into parts, with one fixed mix.

1

Protect · 25%Pays the next 5 to 6 years of expenses, so equity is never touched for daily needs. Arbitrage funds, short term debt funds, corporate bond funds, FDs.

2

Grow: Equity · 65%Builds wealth over 7 years and more. Flexicap, midcap, focused and small cap funds, or SIFs and PMS for larger tickets.

3

Grow: Gold · 10%The shock absorber. It often rises when equity falls. Gold ETF or gold fund of funds.

There is one rule: when a part moves too far from its target share, bring it back.

When equity crashes, its share of the portfolio falls. The rule then moves money from Protect and Gold into equity, so you buy when prices are low. When equity runs up, its share rises. The rule then takes some profit and refills Protect.

Most investors do the opposite. They stop SIPs in a crash and add money at the top. Protect & Grow takes emotion out with rules written down before the market moves.

The mix is set for each family. A retired couple may hold 40% in Protect. A 35 year old with a strong income may hold only 15%. The example above is for a family with ₹5 crore that spends about ₹25 lakh a year, so ₹1.25 crore in Protect covers five years.

When to act, and when to sit still

We check the portfolio once a quarter. We act only when a part crosses its band. Not on news, not on tips, not after one bad week.

How the bands are set:

1

For a large part (20% or more), the base band is 5 points. For a small part, it is 25% of its own weight.

2

For equity and gold, the bands are uneven on purpose. The top up line is close, so we buy dips early. The trim line is far, so winners run before we book profit.

3

Protect stays even on both sides. Its only job is to stay ready.

When a band breaks, we bring the part all the way back to its target, not just to the edge of the band. And we fix it in the cheapest order:

1

New money firstSIPs, bonuses or a lumpsum go into the part that is short. No selling, no tax.

2

Money coming in anywayFD and bond maturities, dividends.

3

Old units onlySell units held more than 12 months, so gains are taxed at the lower long term rate.

4

The rest, only if neededSell newer units only if the gap is still large.

Research by Vanguard on target date funds found that rebalancing on bands like these, instead of on a fixed calendar, added about 0.15% to 0.25% a year.

Worked example: ₹5 crore through a crash

Take the same ₹5 crore family. Here is what the rules did over three years, compared with a portfolio that was set up the same way and never touched.

Y1

Equity fell 25% and dropped to 56% of the portfolio, below its 61% line. We moved ₹37.4 lakh into equity: ₹25.6 lakh from Protect and ₹11.8 lakh from Gold.

Y2

Equity rose 35% and reached 70%, still inside its band. We did nothing.

Y3

Equity rose 30% and reached 74%, above its 73% line. We trimmed ₹63 lakh: ₹42 lakh into Protect and ₹21 lakh into Gold.

After three years, the Protect & Grow portfolio was about ₹24 lakh ahead of the one that was never rebalanced. Tax on all the moves came to between ₹2 lakh and ₹2.65 lakh, depending on the products used.

Why it works: in year one, the rule bought ₹37.4 lakh of equity at low prices. That money then grew 35% and 30%. The untouched portfolio held the same funds, but had less in equity when the recovery came. In year three, the trim booked part of that profit and refilled Protect, ready for the next fall.

Three honest pointsIn a market that only rises, rebalancing lags a little, because it keeps selling equity. Its payoff comes from falls and recoveries. At this size, most of the gain comes from buying in the crash, not from tax savings. And in real life, the family's ₹25 lakh a year comes out of Protect, so equity is never sold to pay bills in a bad year.
A crash only hurts you if it forces you to sell. Protect & Grow exists so that it never does.Atharva

Making it tax efficient

Rebalancing means selling, and selling means tax. The right products in each part make a real difference.

Equity and arbitrage fundsLong term after 12 months · 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh · short term 20%
Debt funds (bought after 1 Apr 2023)Always taxed at your slab
Gold ETFLong term after 12 months · 12.5% · short term at slab
Gold fund of fundsLong term after 24 months · 12.5% · short term at slab

The same moves from the ₹5 crore example, done with two different product choices. Each sale is placed a few days after the units cross 12 months, because long term needs more than 12 months of holding.

The eight levers, in order of impact:

1

New money firstA gap fixed with SIPs, bonuses or maturities costs zero tax.

2

Arbitrage funds for Protect, if you are in the 30% slabThey are taxed like equity, so long term gains pay 12.5%, not 31.2%. Returns are close to short term debt funds.

3

Gold ETF over gold fund of fundsLong term starts at 12 months instead of 24.

4

Sell only old unitsFunds redeem your oldest units first. If those are under 12 months, wait.

5

Use the ₹1.25 lakh exemption every yearSell equity units with that much gain and buy them back the same day. Your cost rises and future tax falls.

6

Use each family member's exemptionEvery adult gets their own ₹1.25 lakh. Money gifted to a spouse is taxed back in your hands; parents and adult children have no such rule.

7

Set off lossesShort term losses offset any capital gain; long term losses only long term gains. Unused losses carry forward 8 years if you file on time.

8

Let a fund rebalance for youBalanced advantage and multi asset funds move between equity, debt and gold inside the fund. You pay tax only when you finally sell.

Wider bands help too: fewer trades mean fewer taxable sales. Please confirm your own case with your CA, especially if surcharge applies to you.

My take

Mr. Kulkarni and Mr. Patwardhan did nothing wrong in picking funds. One of them was simply unlucky with timing, and had no system to protect him from it.

Nobody can choose when the bad year comes. What you can choose is whether a bad year forces you to sell. That is the entire purpose of Protect & Grow: a safe part that pays the bills, a growth part that is left alone to recover, and a few written rules that do the uncomfortable buying and selling for you.
